Private card faces — look & feel

The agreed private-card face — direction A "Ledger", chosen 2026-08-17 (two alternates were explored and dropped). Real 67 × 44 mm card, front (player-owned) and back (company-owned). Shown at 1.5× for reading; the gallery at the bottom is actual size. Corners are drawn rounded here to preview the finished card; the print generator will emit square corners (rounding happens on paper) — node tools/mockup-privates.mjs square renders it that way. Type is the deck's existing pair — Leftfield Serif for display (as on the board mat and bid boxes), Iowan/Palatino for body — so privates sit in the same family as the certs.

What changed from the current cards: the flat pastel tint is gone (cream stock like the certs; the class color survives as an accent — green = a minor may buy it in, red = majors only). Each ability family gets a line-art sigil, so the box sorts by shape as well as by text. Phase is a tile-colored hex chip instead of an outlined numeral; revenue is a labelled coin, so player-side and company-side revenue can never be confused. The three stacked footer chips collapse into one rail, and the dashed art placeholder is replaced by the sigil watermark (no reserved hole waiting on art).

The face

Cream stock, hairline inner frame, a colored spine carrying the card number and the player-count gate. The function label is the headline — what you scan in a bid box — with the flavor name as an italic subhead. The sigil sits behind the type as a watermark. Quietest and closest to the certificate deck.

Sigil plate — one mark per private

Placeholder line art for all 30, drawn stroke-only on a 24×24 grid so the same path serves the 20mm watermark and the 4.6mm chip on the back. Twins that share a rule still differ by flavor — the two Mail Contracts are a raven and a post horn, the two Pullmans a gilded coach and a winged one. The five permits carry the seal, but on the card itself their region letter is the art. Real art is a later pass (C45-era).

Region permits — the letter is the art

All five permit fronts, at 1.25×. The letter and its color are lifted straight off the map (18dragon.json permit labels), so the card matches what a player reads on the hexes. The headline names the permit outright — Verantum Permit — and the eyebrow carries only the buy-in class. The company-owned back repeats the letter as a small chip.
